Six ideas worth discussing

  1. 1A clear meaning of "forest". The reach of the 2023 change is debated. In 2024 the Supreme Court kept the broad 1996 Godavarman definition in force for now.
  2. 2Open ecological data. Census, survey and EIA baseline data that anyone can download and check.
  3. 3Real public participation. EIA summaries in local languages, with enough time to respond.
  4. 4Value open ecosystems. Treat grasslands, deserts, wetlands and mangroves as living systems, not "wasteland".
  5. 5Count the small things. Monitor insects, pollinators and other invertebrates over the long term.
  6. 6Empower local committees. Give Biodiversity Management Committees funds, training and digital tools.
These reflect ongoing public and expert debate. They are prompts for discussion, not legal advice.
