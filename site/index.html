A small share of land, a huge share of life

2.4%of the world's land is India
7–8%of all recorded species live here
4global hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats, Sundaland
~75%of the world's wild tigers

Worldwide, about 1 million species face extinction. Rich places like India have the most to lose, and the most to gain from acting early.

Sources: MoEFCC (2018); Myers et al. (2000); Qureshi et al. (2023); IPBES (2019)

Eight pressures on India's nature

  1. 01Habitat loss & fragmentation. Roads, railways and mines cut through forests and wildlife corridors.
  2. 02Human–wildlife conflict. Elephants, leopards and people compete for shrinking space.
  3. 03Invasive species. Lantana and Prosopis juliflora crowd out native plants.
  4. 04Climate change. Heatwaves, erratic monsoons and coral bleaching.
  5. 05Dying wetlands, rivers & mangroves. Sewage, encroachment and sand mining.
  6. 06Grasslands & deserts labelled "wasteland". Open land is lost to plantations and projects.
  7. 07Insect & pollinator decline. Pesticides and lost flowers harm bees, and long-term insect data is scarce.
  8. 08Data gaps & silos. Many species are unrecorded, and existing data is hard to access.
Sources: IPBES (2019); Mungi et al. (2020); Sánchez-Bayo & Wyckhuys (2019)
Part 2 · Data in action

Ecological intelligence is a loop

It turns what we observe in nature into timely, evidence-based decisions, then checks whether those decisions worked.

Why faster now? Satellites revisit every few days, AI labels millions of photos, and thousands of citizens report at once. Problems show up in months instead of decades.

Sources: Hansen et al. (2013); Norouzzadeh et al. (2018); Sullivan et al. (2009)
Evidence → action
1 · Observe
camera traps, satellites, apps
2 · Organise
open databases & maps
3 · Analyse
statistics, AI, models
4 · Act
protect, restore, alert, legislate
5 · Re-measure
did it work? loop again

Counting tigers with 47 million photos

3,167tigers photographed and identified one by one in 2022 (a minimum count)
ProblemOld counts relied on pugmarks (footprints), which were unreliable. DataCamera traps at 32,588 locations took about 47 million photos. InsightStripes are unique, so software tells individual tigers apart, and AI sorts the photos. ActionThe maps guide which reserves and corridors get protection.
Sources: Qureshi et al. (2023); Norouzzadeh et al. (2018)

Example 3 of 4 · Grasslands & desert
←11 / 17→

When field data reached the Supreme Court

~150Great Indian Bustards are estimated to be left in the wild
DataResearchers walked along power lines in the Thar and counted the birds killed in collisions. InsightHeavy bustards can't see the wires or turn away in time. ActionThe Supreme Court ordered bird diverters and underground lines. Its 2024 ruling also recognised a right to be free from the harms of climate change.
Sources: Uddin et al. (2021); M. K. Ranjitsinh v. Union of India (2021, 2024)
Example 4 of 4 · From space
←12 / 17→

Green from space doesn't always mean healthy

24.62%of India was under forest and tree cover in 2021
DataLandsat maps tree cover change at 30 m detail. The Forest Survey of India reports every two years. InsightTree cover hides damage below the canopy: invasive Lantana has spread through more than 40% of the forests. ActionClear invasives and restore native plants where models predict the highest risk.
Sources: Hansen et al. (2013); Forest Survey of India (2021); Mungi et al. (2020)

50 years of laws for nature

1972Wildlife (Protection) ActProtected areas and species lists. Updated in 2022.
1976Articles 48A & 51A(g)Protecting nature becomes a duty of the State and of every citizen.
1980Forest (Conservation) ActCentral approval needed before forest land is used for anything else. Amended in 2023.
1986Environment (Protection) ActThe umbrella law. The EIA (Environmental Impact Assessment) rules of 2006 come under it.
2002Biological Diversity ActLocal committees and People's Biodiversity Registers. Amended in 2023.
2006Forest Rights ActRights for forest-dwelling communities and a voice for the gram sabha.
2010National Green TribunalA special court for environmental cases.
2017Wetlands RulesStates must map, notify and manage their wetlands.
Simplified for awareness, not legal advice. Full texts: indiacode.nic.in
Sundarbans mangroves shield millions of people from cyclones, and they depend on strong coastal rules
Part 3 · Laws
←14 / 17→

Six ideas worth discussing

  1. 1A clear meaning of "forest". The reach of the 2023 change is debated. In 2024 the Supreme Court kept the broad 1996 Godavarman definition in force for now.
  2. 2Open ecological data. Census, survey and EIA baseline data that anyone can download and check.
  3. 3Real public participation. EIA summaries in local languages, with enough time to respond.
  4. 4Value open ecosystems. Treat grasslands, deserts, wetlands and mangroves as living systems, not "wasteland".
  5. 5Count the small things. Monitor insects, pollinators and other invertebrates over the long term.
  6. 6Empower local committees. Give Biodiversity Management Committees funds, training and digital tools.
These reflect ongoing public and expert debate. They are prompts for discussion, not legal advice.
